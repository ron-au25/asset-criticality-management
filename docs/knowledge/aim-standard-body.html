Source: Asset Inventory Management (AIM) Standard, version 1.2 (National Operations, Property Management). Internal use. This article condenses the standard; the standard itself is the authority.

1. Purpose and scope

The business owns and manages buildings across Australia (mainly shopping centres) that need regular maintenance, servicing and inspection, and in some cases capital investment. The AIM Standard sets the minimum standard for keeping accurate asset inventory data in the Facilities Management (FM) business systems. It is the baseline for setting, managing and monitoring service and maintenance, and for capital planning.

  • Which assets must be tracked in the Asset Inventory Register for each property.
  • Which assets are critical to the business and need closer monitoring, especially where failure affects operations or regulatory obligations.
  • The key data needed to assess business impact, manage maintenance cost and plan capital investment.

Applies to building systems, equipment and other inventory items owned by the business and physically located at owned or managed buildings. Items may be subject to servicing, maintenance, repair or inspection under property management obligations, and to programmatic capital investment.

4. Asset categories

All items are assigned a category, which defines the level of management required.

CategoryWhat it meansExamples
Category 1
  • Major equipment managed as an individual item with its own asset ID (which may be needed to connect to corporate networks).
  • Planned and unplanned maintenance costs are captured against the item.
  • Must be assessed under the criticality assessment; may carry associated compliance obligations.
  • Ideally identified against a Space or Tenancy Unit.
Chiller – air cooled, escalator, fire indicator panel
Category 2
  • Medium to low value equipment, or high volumes of each asset that make individual tracking onerous.
  • Record the volume of each asset as an attribute.
  • May be assessed for criticality and carry statutory or regulatory maintenance obligations.
  • Maintenance costs are not required against the item.
  • Ideally identified against a Space; can be identified against the building.
Electrical air breaker, security door, fire hose reel
Category 2N (networked)
  • Assets that need a connection to the corporate network or Wi-Fi certificates.
  • Recorded as a parent-child relationship: the parent is used for maintenance tracking and cost management; each individual asset is a Category 2N child with a network ID.
Security cameras, digital directories
Category 3
  • Captured purely to record an item as owned equipment in the building inventory.
  • May not be subject to any maintenance or inspection regime.
  • Record the volume as an attribute.
Stab proof vest, office furniture

5. Criticality assessment

Items that would have a significant impact if they failed, or that are more likely to fail because of age or condition, get a more stringent maintenance and monitoring regime. The assessment decides which items are recorded as Critical. Category 1 items must be assessed; Category 2 items may be.

  1. Impact assessment – score six impact areas.
  2. Condition assessment – score maintenance history and age.
  3. Criticality score – combine impact and condition on the matrix.

5.1 Impact assessment

Score each of the six criteria: Not applicable (0), Minor (1), Moderate (2) or Major (3). Add the six scores for the overall impact score (maximum 18).

Impact areaMinor = 1Moderate = 2Major = 3

DisruptionUnscheduled maintenance with no impact on operations and tenants.Unscheduled maintenance with a limited impact on operations and tenants.Loss of ability to sustain operations and/or temporary closure of part or all of the centre.

Ranges are shown as printed in the standard; the value 7 appears in both Minor and Moderate.

Example: HVAC actuator fire-smoke scores Environmental 1, Health and safety 2, Financial 3, Disruption 2, Reputational 2, Legal 2 = 12, a Moderate impact.

Generic and centre-specific assessment. A generic impact assessment is applied to each centre's register as a baseline. Centre teams should run their own impact assessments at least every 24 months, because local risk differs (for example, electrical failure matters less where a back-up generator exists).

11. Service and maintenance monitoring

Repair-versus-replace decisions need accurate frequency and cost data for planned and unplanned work. That is only achievable for major equipment held as individual assets, that is Category 1.

  1. Planned maintenance schedules cover all like items at each building, at the frequency maintenance occurs. Where a schedule covers more than one item, the planned cost per item is held as an asset attribute.
  2. Unplanned or reactive tasks are linked to the item worked on. Every reactive task must carry an accurate cost to avoid spend discrepancy.

12. Facilities management business systems

Service providers may keep detailed asset and maintenance data in their own systems, but the data covered by this Standard must be maintained inside the FM business systems. Where provider systems are used, National Operations and PM Business Solutions run a data transfer and alignment at least every 90 days. The preferred maintenance management system supports criticality and associated compliance by:

  • Every item has an attribute recording its criticality score (1 to 9).
  • Schedules for critical items (scores 6 and 9) are created under the task type PPM – Critical.
  • Every item has an attribute recording whether Associated Compliance is True or False.
  • Schedules for items with Associated Compliance are created under the task type PPM – Associated Compliance.
  • Critical and associated-compliance activities must be completed within 30 days of the task generation date.
